# RE_046 · Dense meshes with Nanite disabled

| Category | Severity | Fires while | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rendering | Medium | Any time — it scans the loaded level | 30 FPS and 60 FPS |

## What it means

There are meshes in the level dense enough that Nanite would normally be worth enabling, and it isn't. At that density, every rendering pass that includes them rasterizes the whole mesh the traditional way.

## When it fires

| Measurement | Looks for | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Meshes missing Nanite | High | Meshes in the loaded level dense enough for Nanite to be worth enabling, without it enabled |

## Why it happens

Nanite exists to remove the cost of rendering very dense meshes at every distance and in every pass. Leaving it off on a mesh dense enough to benefit means paying the traditional rasterization cost for geometry that was specifically designed to avoid it.

## How to fix it

1. **Enable Nanite on the mesh** — Static Mesh Editor → Nanite Settings → Enable Nanite Support.
2. **Check that the material is compatible.** World Position Offset and translucency limit or prevent Nanite.
3. **If the project doesn't use Nanite, generating LODs is the alternative.**

## How to confirm the fix

Run **Scan level** again in the Level composition section. Once the meshes flagged "Nanite off" drop to zero, the rule stops firing and its row in Diagnostics is no longer marked active.
