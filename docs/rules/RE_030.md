# RE_030 · High triangle count without GPU-bound — possible culling/visibility cost

| Category | Severity | Fires while | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rendering | Medium | Playing | 30 FPS and 60 FPS |

## What it means

A high triangle count usually points to GPU cost, but here the bottleneck is the render thread instead. That suggests the CPU cost of deciding what to draw — culling, visibility, LOD selection — is the cause, not rasterization itself.

## When it fires

All of these are true at the same time, averaged over one second:

| Measurement | Looks for | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Triangles drawn | High | Triangles submitted this frame, counted once per rendering pass |
| GPU bound | No | The GPU is not what limits the frame |
| Render thread time | High | Time the render thread spends building the frame for the GPU |

## Why it happens

Deciding what to draw has its own CPU cost, separate from actually drawing it. A scene with a lot of geometry spread across many actors can make Occlusion Culling and Hierarchical LOD evaluation expensive even before the GPU touches a single triangle.

## How to fix it

1. **Review the cost of Occlusion Culling and Hierarchical LOD** for the current amount of geometry.
2. **Profile the Visibility/Gather phase of the render thread with Unreal Insights.**

## How to confirm the fix

Watch **Render Thread** relative to **Triangles** in Live Metrics. Once the render thread falls back under the rule's threshold, the rule stops firing and its row in Diagnostics is no longer marked active.
