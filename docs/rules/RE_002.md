# RE_002 · Excessive triangle count without Nanite

| Category | Severity | Fires while | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rendering | High | Playing | 30 FPS and 60 FPS |

## What it means

The scene is pushing more triangles through the traditional rasterizer than it can process in a single frame, and Nanite — the feature built specifically to remove this ceiling — is not doing the work. The render thread is over budget and the GPU is the limit.

## When it fires

All of these are true at the same time:

| Measurement | Looks for | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Triangles drawn | High | Triangles submitted this frame, counted once per rendering pass (prepass, base pass, shadow cascades), so the total is higher than the geometry actually in the level |
| Render thread time | High | Time the render thread spends building the frame for the GPU |
| GPU bound | Yes | The GPU, not the game thread, is what limits the frame |

## Why it happens

High-density meshes — photogrammetry scans, ZBrush exports, CAD imports — rasterized at full detail through every pass multiply this count fast. Nanite exists precisely so this triangle count stops mattering; without it, every one of those triangles is rasterized the traditional way, at every distance from the camera.

## How to fix it

1. **Enable Nanite on eligible high-poly Static Meshes.** Open the mesh, and under **Nanite Settings** check **Enable Nanite Support**.
2. **Set up aggressive LODs for meshes outside the near camera range**, so distant geometry sheds detail instead of staying at full density.
3. **Audit imported assets for unnecessary density.** A scanned or sculpted asset brought in without decimation is the most common source of this.

## How to confirm the fix

Play again and watch **Triangles** and **Render Thread** in Live Metrics. Once the triangle count drops back under the rule's threshold or the render thread returns under its budget, the rule stops firing and its row in Diagnostics is no longer marked active.
