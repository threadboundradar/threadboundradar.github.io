# RE_004 · Critical shading cost with few draw calls (30 FPS)

| Category | Severity | Fires while | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rendering | High | Playing | 30 FPS and 60 FPS |

## What it means

The same shading-cost pattern as RE_003, but severe enough to miss even a 30 FPS budget. A handful of draw calls is consuming the whole frame — this is not a marginal case.

## When it fires

All of these are true at the same time, averaged over one second:

| Measurement | Looks for | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| GPU time | High | Time the GPU spends on this frame |
| Draw calls | Low | Commands sent to the GPU this frame |
| GPU bound | Yes | The GPU, not the game thread, is what limits the frame |

## Why it happens

The mechanism is the same as RE_003: shading cost scales with material complexity and with how many times each pixel is shaded, not with the number of draw calls. At this level, the shaders or the translucency stack in use are expensive enough to blow through even a 30 FPS budget on their own.

## How to fix it

1. **Check the complexity of your most-used materials** in Material Stats or the Shader Complexity view.
2. **Reduce overlapping translucency layers or simplify the costliest shaders.**
3. **Profile with Unreal Insights** to see exactly which rendering pass is consuming the time.

## How to confirm the fix

Watch **GPU Time** in Live Metrics. Once it returns under the 30 FPS frame budget, the rule stops firing and its row in Diagnostics is no longer marked active.
